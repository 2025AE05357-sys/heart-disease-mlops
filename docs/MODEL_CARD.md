## Intended use

Demonstrating an MLOps pipeline for a binary classifier of coronary heart disease (angiographic narrowing > 50% in any major vessel) from 13 routine clinical measurements. Out of scope: diagnosis, triage, screening or any use on real patients.

## Training data

UCI Heart Disease, Cleveland Clinic Foundation: 242 patients for training and cross-validation, 61 held out (stratified, seed 42). The Hungarian, Swiss and VA Long Beach databases were never used for training or tuning; they are external validation sets. ## Limitations

| Limitation | Evidence and consequence |
|---|---|
| Small, old sample | 242 training patients from the 1980s; practice, populations and test protocols have changed since |
| Does not transfer across hospitals | External ROC-AUC 0.70–0.89 against 0.95 on the hold-out set: case mix, prevalence and recording practice differ by site |
| Missing tests at other sites | `ca` (fluoroscopy) and `thal` (thallium scan) are missing for most external patients and are imputed with Cleveland's most common value |
| Sex as a predictor | Male sex carries a large positive weight. This reflects the cohort (two thirds male, higher disease rate in men), not a causal effect; see the slice above |
| Calibration | Probabilities are calibrated to Cleveland's prevalence; at a site with a different prevalence the probabilities shift even when the ranking holds |

## Risk bands (API)

Besides the class, the API returns a band: low (< 0.3), moderate (0.3–0.6), high (≥ 0.6). The bands are a reading aid, not a clinical risk score.

## Monitoring

Production metrics track request volume and latency, the predicted-class mix and the probability distribution, and the distribution of age, cholesterol and maximum heart rate. A shift towards the Swiss or VA profile (older patients, lower heart rates, more asymptomatic chest pain) is the expected failure mode and the retraining trigger.
